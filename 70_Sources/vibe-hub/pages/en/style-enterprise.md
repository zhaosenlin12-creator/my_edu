# B2B Corporate Site

You might say

This site is for business customers, so it needs to feel professional, steady, and trustworthy.

**Build confidence through stable structure, concrete evidence, and real business context**·A B2B corporate site is a website type for organizational buying, not a fixed visual style. It often supports a longer decision involving several people, so clear navigation, industry context, customer stories, compliance, and service information must work together to establish trust. The visual language can feel powerful, but every ambitious claim needs evidence from data, a case, or a real product capability.

Design Principles

- **Design the information architecture first**: products, industries, resources, and company information must be easy to find
- **Support every claim with evidence**: use verifiable numbers, certifications, customer outcomes, and service scope
- **Use real operating context** instead of generic business imagery so visitors can recognize their work
- **Professional does not mean rigid**: type, photography, and motion can express a brand without slowing understanding

### When to use it

- **B2B software, industry, finance, and professional services** where trust matters more than impulse

  Operating-information field**Verifiable details**Security · Scale · Support
- **Products serving several industries or roles** that require durable navigation for complex content

  **Scope**Service area

  **Methodology**Team metric

  **Time period**Support details
- **Purchases involving several stakeholders**, where cases, security, and support need equal visibility
- **Brands operating across markets** that need a layout system able to scale across languages and business lines

### When NOT to use it

- **Rely on grand claims**: leading, empowering, and transforming say little without evidence

  **World-class synergy**Big words, no proof or next step
- **Turn the homepage into an org chart**; internal departments are not the customer's problem-solving path
- **Put every section in a dark blue box**; order creates professionalism, not one color alone
- **Treat a logo wall as a complete case study**; explain the problem, work, and understandable outcome
