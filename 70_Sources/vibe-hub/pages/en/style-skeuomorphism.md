# Skeuomorphism

You might say

Give it that early iPhone look, where buttons look like real buttons and notes look like actual notebooks.

**Use familiar real-world form and material to explain a digital control**·Skeuomorphism makes digital objects resemble physical ones through shape, texture, light, and behavior. It can make an unfamiliar interaction easier to understand or give a focused experience character. Literal leather, wood, and metal on every surface quickly add noise and age poorly.

Design Principles

- Use the physical reference to clarify an interaction
- Keep light, material, and depth internally consistent
- Preserve readable labels and digital accessibility
- Apply detail to a small number of important objects
- Let digital behavior stay efficient even when the appearance is familiar

### When to use it

- Music and instrument interfaces

  Teaching older adults to use a tabletClick here to send a voice message
- Games and playful tools

  DiaryA retro journaling app can use material cues to support its theme.
- A focused control with a useful physical analogy

  WoodStringsRealistic control panels for cameras, recorders, and synthesizers
- Brand experiences built around craft or material

  A strip of leather adds a small accent to an otherwise modern light interface.

### When NOT to use it

- Cover an entire work tool in decorative textures

  Body text sits on a textured background
- Copy a physical limitation that makes the digital task slower

  One interface uses multiple material imagesAssess asset costs
- Use realistic shading without clear interaction states

  Report CenterThe material treatment does not fit the task.

  Efficiency tools should prioritize clear data and controls.
- Assume a physical reference is familiar to every audience

History

2007.01

**The iPhone brings skeuomorphism to touchscreens**

Slide to unlock, page-turning calendars, and other physical references helped first-generation touchscreen users understand unfamiliar actions.

2010—2012

**The peak of rich digital material**

iOS 5 and 6 used yellow paper, wooden shelves, green felt, leather, and other detailed scenes across built-in apps.

2013.09

**iOS 7 turns toward flat design**

Apple removed most leather, linen, and realistic texture, and the wider industry quickly moved toward flatter interfaces.

2020s

**Material returns as an accent**

Neumorphism and spatial platforms brought back glass, depth, and physical material, usually as a supporting effect rather than the whole interface.
